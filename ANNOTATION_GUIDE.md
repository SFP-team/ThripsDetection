# How to annotate the blueberry leaf squares

This guide is for the person marking tiles. It explains what you are looking at, what each button means, and how to handle squares that are not obvious. You are not scoring a plant from 1 to 5. You are describing what is in one square.

The first set is 36 rover photos of young blueberry plants. The computer has already cut each plant into overlapping 512 pixel squares and kept the ones that look like foliage. Your job is to mark those squares.

---

## What we are trying to see

Chilli thrips are tiny. You will almost never see the insect in these pictures. What you can see is **damage on the young leaves at the top of the plant**. That young flush is what scouts score in the field.

Typical thrips injury on new blueberry growth:

- A dull **bronze** or **silver** sheen on the leaf, as if the surface was scraped or dusted. It is not the same as a healthy shiny green leaf.
- **Cupping**: the leaf edges roll up or the blade makes a bowl.
- **Crinkle or twist**: the blade is puckered, wavy, or pulled out of a flat shape.
- On a badly hit plant, the youngest tips can look dark, dry, or dead.

We only score that story on **new growth**. Old hard leaves, the white grow tube, the pot, a tag, and the sky do not get a thrips injured / healthy mark. If you mark those as injured, the later plant score will be wrong.

---

## What you see on the screen

There are two pictures.

The **large square on the left** is the tile you are marking. This is the only thing the buttons apply to. Do not mark it based on a leaf you can see on the whole plant but that is not inside this square.

The **picture on the right** is the whole plant, with the current square outlined. Use it to understand where you are. The top of the plant is usually new growth. The lower canopy and the sides are often older leaves. The white cylinder is the grow tube. If you are unsure whether a leaf is young or old, look at its place on the plant and at the leaves next to it.

Along the bottom is a strip of all squares from **this same plant**. You can click one to jump. A mark you already saved will show in the strip.

You also have a name on Home. That name is stored with each mark. Type it before you start.

---

## The order of questions

Every square starts with: **what tissue is this?**

1. **New growth**
2. **Old leaves**
3. **Not a leaf**

If you choose old leaves or not a leaf, the square is finished. The app saves it as skip for thrips scoring. You do not answer hurt or curl.

If you choose new growth, you must answer two more things:

- Is this new growth **healthy**, **injured**, or **skip**?
- If it is healthy or injured: is there **curl yes** or **curl no**?

The square is not saved until that chain is complete. If you only press New growth and walk away, that square is not in the database yet.

---

## 1. New growth

Choose this when the square is mostly **young flush**: the soft, recently opened leaves at the top of the bush.

What it usually looks like:

- Thinner, more tender blades than the older canopy.
- Often a brighter lime or yellow-green, or a pale silvery green if thrips have already hit it.
- Sits high on the plant in the context picture. These are the shoots a scout would pinch and look at.

Pale or silvered young leaves are still new growth. Do not send them to Not a leaf because they are not a rich dark green. Those pale tips are often the exact damaged flush we care about.

If the square is a mix of young leaves and something else, decide by what you would use for a thrips call:

- If there is a real patch of young flush you can judge, treat the square as **new growth** and then use the mixed rule below.
- If the young bit is a sliver you cannot honestly judge, and the rest is old leaf or tube, use Old leaves or Not a leaf.

---

## 2. Old leaves

Choose this when the square is **mature canopy or old wood**, not the young top flush.

What it usually looks like:

- Thicker, darker, leathery leaves.
- Lower or outer on the plant in the context picture.
- Sometimes brown woody stem, older petioles, or tired leaves that are not the current flush.

Old leaves can have spots, dust, sun scorch, or wear. **That is not what we are scoring.** Do not mark old leaves as injured even if they look ugly. Thrips scoring for this project is on new growth only. Old leaves save as skip.

If you are torn between new and old, use the context picture. A leaf that is clearly part of last season’s or the hardened mid-canopy is old. A leaf that is part of the current soft top is new.

---

## 3. Not a leaf

Choose this when the square is **not foliage we should score**, even if a little plant tissue is in the corner.

Typical cases:

- The white plastic **grow tube**
- Pot, tray, tag, stake, string
- Mulch, soil, gravel
- Sky or the bright field background
- Empty blur, heavy glare with no readable leaf
- A square that is almost only stem or tube wall

A little leaf overlapping the tube is common. If the readable content is the tube and you cannot judge flush, use Not a leaf. If the square is clearly young leaves and the tube is only an edge, use New growth.

Not a leaf also saves as skip. It is not “injured.”

---

## After New growth: healthy, injured, or skip

This question is only about **thrips-looking damage on the young tissue in this square**. It is not a 1–5. It is not “does the whole plant look bad.”

### Healthy

Use healthy when the new growth in the square looks **clean for thrips**.

- Normal green or lime new leaves.
- A natural slight wave that still looks like a normal young leaf, not a cupped or crinkled injury.
- **Lime crinkle with no bronze and no cup:** some young blueberry leaves are puckered or lime-colored without looking scraped or silvered. If you do not see bronze, silver, or a real cup, mark **healthy**. You can still say curl yes if the shape is cupped or crinkled. Healthy plus curl yes is allowed and useful.

Do not mark healthy just because the plant “looks okay” in the small context picture if this square itself is bronze or silver.

### Injured

Use injured when the new growth shows **thrips-type damage**:

- Bronze or silver sheen on the young blade
- Cupped young leaves
- Crinkle that comes with that scraped or metallic look
- Dark or dying young tips that fit with the rest of the flush damage

**Mixed new growth = injured.** If the square has some clean young leaf and some clearly damaged young leaf, mark injured. We would rather catch the damage than average it away. The later plant score counts how much of the young flush looks hurt. A mixed square is a hurt square.

Do not mark injured for:

- Old-leaf wear (you should have chosen Old leaves)
- Tube, pot, or dirt (Not a leaf)
- A healthy lime leaf that is only a bit wrinkled, with no bronze, silver, or cup

### Skip (on new growth)

Use skip when the square really is new growth but you **cannot honestly tell**. Examples: heavy blur, a tiny scrap of flush, a shadow or glare that hides the surface, or you cannot tell bronze from a lighting glare.

Skip should be uncommon. If you can see the leaf surface, prefer healthy or injured. Do not use skip because the plant is “in between 2 and 3.” There is no 2 or 3 on a square.

---

## Curl yes or curl no

Ask this only after healthy or injured on new growth. Curl is **shape**, not color.

**Curl yes** if the new growth in the square is cupped, rolled, twisted, or crinkled in a way that the blade is not lying flat. A bowl-shaped young leaf is yes. A twisted tip is yes. Strong puckering is yes.

**Curl no** if the young blades are basically flat, even if they are bronze or silver. Color can be injured with curl no. That is a real and useful combination.

A healthy flat leaf is healthy + curl no.  
A healthy but cupped lime leaf (no bronze) is healthy + curl yes.  
A silver cupped leaf is injured + curl yes.  
A silver flat leaf is injured + curl no.

You must pick yes or no. The app will not save a healthy or injured new-growth square without curl.

---

## How to use the whole-plant picture

Use it to answer “where am I?” not to copy a whole-plant grade onto the square.

- High in the outline, soft lime or silver tips: lean new growth.
- Mid or low, darker thicker leaves: lean old leaves.
- White ring or wall: tube, often Not a leaf unless leaves fill the square.
- The outline is the only region that counts for the buttons.

If the close-up looks confusing, glance at the plant, then look back at the square and decide from the square.

---

## Common situations

**Silver or pale top leaves.** Still new growth. Often injured. Do not throw them out as Not a leaf because they are not dark green.

**One damaged leaf and one clean young leaf in the same square.** New growth, injured. Then curl from the young tissue as a whole: if either the damaged part or the clean part is clearly cupped or crinkled, curl yes. If everything young is flat, curl no.

**Mostly tube, a few leaves.** If you can clearly judge those leaves as young flush, mark New growth and continue. If you cannot, Not a leaf.

**Brown crispy old leaf.** Old leaves, not injured.

**You clicked the wrong tissue.** Esc clears an unfinished square. Z undoes the last saved square. You can also open Review and click a square to fix it. After you save the fix, you go back to Review.

**You want to give the square a 3.** Do not. Healthy, injured, or skip is the whole injury question.

---

## Keys

- `1` `2` `3` first choose tissue (new growth, old leaves, not a leaf). After new growth, the same keys choose healthy, injured, skip.
- `Y` curl yes, `N` curl no
- `Z` undo the last saved mark
- `Esc` clear the draft on this square if it is not saved yet
- Left and right arrows move along this plant’s strip

---

## Saving and coming back

A finished square is written to this computer as soon as the last required click happens. Old leaves and not a leaf save on that one click. New growth saves when you finish skip, or when you finish healthy/injured plus curl.

Close the browser whenever you want. Open the same address, type your name if the box is empty, and press **Continue last session**. Stay on this computer. Starting a new folder upload starts a new session and does not add marks to the old one.

When you want to check yourself, open **Review**. You will see four columns: new growth healthy, new growth injured, old leaves, and not a leaf. If a healthy column is full of silver cupped tips, those were marked wrong. Click one and fix it.

---

## Short recap

You are describing one square, not grading the bush 1 to 5.

New growth is the only tissue that gets healthy / injured / curl.  
Old leaves and not a leaf are skip.  
Bronze, silver, cup, or thrips-like crinkle on young flush is injured.  
A mix of hurt and clean young flush is injured.  
Lime crinkle with no bronze and no cup is healthy.  
Curl is shape. Color is injury. They are two different answers.
