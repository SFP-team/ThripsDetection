    How to annotate

    Label visible damage on current new growth. Do not look for insects. Do not rate the whole plant on a square. Do not score old-leaf damage.

    Question 1

    What tissue is this?

    Do not use a percent of the whole square to choose tissue. Ask whether you can judge current new growth in this square.

    New growth

    A real, readable patch of young flush is visible. A few young leaves in a corner is enough. It does not need to fill most of the square.

    Old leaves

    The useful foliage is mature, and the young bit is too small, too blurry, or too cut off to judge.

    No leaf

    Tube, pot, soil, sky, background, stem only, heavy blur, or no useful leaf tissue.

    Mixed new and old leaves: if the young flush is judgeable, choose New growth. Then ignore the old leaves and mark Healthy, Mild, or Severe only on that new growth. The ~50% split is for damage on the young tissue, not for how much of the tile is young.

    Example: 70% old canopy and 30% bronzed young tips. Choose New growth, then Mild or Severe from those tips. Do not choose Old leaves or the model never sees the damage.

    Example: almost all old leaf plus a sliver of yellow you cannot read. Choose Old leaves. Do not guess.

    Question 2 · New growth only

    How much of the visible new growth is damaged?

    The percent is of the young tissue you can judge in this square, not of the whole tile and not of the plant.

    Healthy · none

    No convincing injury. Pale lime color, natural folding, soft leaves, or slight developmental crinkling alone are not damage.

    Mild · under ~50%

    Damage is clearly present, but most visible new growth still looks reasonably normal. Localized silvering, bronzing, scarring, or slight abnormal deformation.

    Severe · ~50% or more

    Damage dominates the visible new growth. Extensive discoloration, strong cupping or twisting, collapse, major deformation, or a dead young terminal.

    Unsure

    Judgeable new growth is present, but the evidence is genuinely ambiguous. Use this instead of guessing between healthy, mild, and severe.

    Mild: under about half of the visible new growth is damaged. Normal tissue still dominates.

    Severe: about half or more of the visible new growth is damaged. Damage dominates.

    Common difficult cases

    Use the same rules every time

    Pale or folded young leaves

    Do not call them damaged from color or folding alone. If there is no convincing injury, choose Healthy.

    Silvering without curl

    Curl is not required. Localized silvering can be Mild. Extensive or intense silvering can be Severe.

    Curl without discoloration

    Clearly abnormal but limited deformation can be Mild. Strong deformation that dominates the cluster can be Severe.

    Dead or dark terminal

    There is no extra checkbox. If a current young tip is clearly dead and that damage dominates the tile, choose Severe.

    Mixed new and old in one square

    Do not pick the tissue that fills more of the tile. If the young flush is judgeable, choose New growth and score only that flush. Choose Old leaves only when the young bit cannot be judged.

    Old-leaf spots

    Do not grade them as new-growth damage. Choose Old leaves when there is no judgeable new growth.

    Ambiguous new growth

    Choose Unsure when the young tissue is visible but severity cannot be decided. Choose No leaf only when useful leaf tissue cannot be assessed.
